## Post Body:

Hi HN,

I built the Reality Constraint Fuzzer (RCF), a framework that discovers laws by testing which constraints survive maximal pressure. Instead of assuming laws and deriving consequences, it inverts the process: **only what survives becomes fundamental**.

**Core idea:** Reality isn't what exists—it's what survives pressure.

The system:
- Runs constraint tournaments where multiple rule sets compete
- Applies structured stress (entropy storms, memory pressure, intent gradients)
- Tracks which constraints survive longest across dimensional projections
- Extracts "Irreducible Law Kernels" (ILKs)—minimal constraint sets explaining all survivals

**Why this matters:**
Most simulation frameworks assume physics and derive behavior. RCF does the opposite—it discovers which constraints *allow* reality by testing survival under adversarial conditions.

**Example run:**
- Start with ~10 competing constraint sets
- Apply 200 volleys of increasing pressure
- Most collapse within 50 volleys (collision, crystallization, envelope contraction)
- Survivors reveal fundamental vs decorative laws
- Extract scaling exponents, universality classes, collapse topologies

**Philosophical grounding:**
This embodies survival-based epistemology: laws aren't true because they're elegant—they're elegant because they survived. It's Popper's falsificationism as a computational engine.

**Implementations:**
- Python (with matplotlib visualizations)
- React/TypeScript (interactive web interface)
- Kotlin (JVM implementation)

**Novel features:**
- Constraint personas (Judge/Trickster/Guardian) narrate violations
- Entropy weather (calm periods + periodic storms)
- Collapse cartography (maps forbidden phase space regions)
- Survivor genealogy (tracks constraint-satisfying lineages)
- LawScore metric: (survival_time × dimensional_persistence) / (variance + sensitivity)

**Current state:**
This is a research prototype demonstrating the concept. It doesn't yet rediscover known physics (though that's the goal), but it does successfully:
- Distinguish robust from brittle constraints
- Identify minimal constraint sets
- Reveal emergent universality classes
- Generate falsifiable predictions about constraint dominance

**Open questions I'm exploring:**
1. Can this rediscover conservation laws from pure constraint tournaments?
2. Do universality classes emerge that match known physics?
3. Can meta-laws predict which constraints will dominate?
4. Is there a "constraint grammar" underlying physical law?

**Limitations I'm honest about:**
- Can't prove uniqueness of reality
- Can't escape Gödel-like undecidability
- Doesn't replace experimental physics
- Currently tests preprogrammed constraints (not generating novel ones)

## HN Comment Response Templates

**"This is just curve fitting"**
Fair question. Key difference: I'm not fitting to observed data. The constraints compete in isolation under adversarial pressure. Survival determines fundamentality *before* comparison to physics. It's discovery through elimination, not optimization toward a target.

**"How is this different from genetic algorithms?"**
Genetic algorithms optimize toward a fitness function you define. RCF has no fitness function—only survival time under pressure. The "goal" emerges from what doesn't collapse, not what we reward. It's selection without teleology.

**"Can this actually discover new physics?"**
Honest answer: Not yet. Current version tests which preprogrammed constraints survive longest. Next step: meta-constraint generation (constraints that create constraints). If we can bootstrap from minimal primitives to complex laws through pure survival pressure, that would be discovery.

**"What's the practical application?"**
Three potential uses:
1. Constraint satisfaction research (which rule sets are robust?)
2. Symbolic regression alternative (discover governing equations through survival)
3. Philosophical toolkit (computational falsificationism)

Currently it's more research instrument than product.

**"The philosophy sounds like pseudoscience"**
I get that. Two clarifications:
1. This is computational philosophy, not physics (yet)
2. The claim is methodological: "survival under pressure is necessary for fundamentality"—not metaphysical: "reality IS survival"

It's operationalism + evolutionary epistemology implemented as code.

**"Why not just use physics simulators?"**
Physics simulators assume the laws you're trying to discover. RCF inverts this: start with minimal constraints, apply maximum pressure, see what survives. It's constraint archaeology, not forward simulation.

**"Your LawScore metric seems arbitrary"**
Fair. It's (survival_time × dimensional_persistence) / (variance + sensitivity). Design choice: reward longevity and cross-dimensional stability, penalize instability and perturbation sensitivity. Open to better formulations—this is v1.

**"How do you prevent overfitting to your perturbation schedule?"**
Good catch. Current mitigation: test under multiple perturbation schedules, verify LawScore ordering remains stable. Future: adversarial perturbation generation (worst-case stress testing). It's like fuzzing but for physical laws.
